SmartCity360 – A Smart City Management Platform 
SmartCity360 is a digital solution that manages city infrastructure, devices, services, and resident 
interactions. It integrates systems such as waste management, energy monitoring, sensor networks, and 
public administration. 
As a senior developer at UrbanTech Solutions, your role is to architect maintainable, efficient, and secure 
components using design patterns. 
In this assessment, you will work on five parts (A, B, C, D, and E), each focusing on different 
components of the SmartCity360 system and requiring you to apply appropriate object-oriented design 
patterns to solve real-world problems. Part F provides instructions for the final presentation and report. 
Part A - Dynamic Security Wrappers for Data Streams 
SmartCity360 must secure data streams from energy meters with layers such as encryption, authentication, 
and compression, which should be addable without altering the core logic. 
1. Identify and justify the most suitable design pattern for this requirement. (3 marks) 
2. Explain how the pattern supports flexible and layered enhancements to object functionality. (4 
marks) 
3. Evaluate the long-term benefits of this pattern in terms of reusability and system extensibility. (8 
marks) 
(Total = 15 marks) 
Part B - Hierarchical Structure of Smart Zones 
City zones (city → district → street → building) need to be represented hierarchically so that commands 
like “shutdown power” or “report usage” can be applied uniformly at any level. 
1. Identify the most appropriate design pattern to model such a structure. (3 marks) 
2. Provide a UML class diagram to demonstrate the selected design pattern’s mechanism. (4 marks) 
3. Discuss its usefulness for managing a large and growing city infrastructure. (8 marks) 
(Total = 15 marks) 
Part C - Escalation of Citizen Complaints 
When a resident submits a complaint, the system should escalate it from local staff to supervisors to city 
managers depending on severity. 
1. Identify the most appropriate pattern to handle dynamic escalation paths. (3 marks) 
2. Explain how this pattern enables decoupled request handling. (4 marks) 
3. Evaluate its benefits and drawbacks in terms of flexibility and traceability. (8 marks) 
(Total = 15 marks) 
Part D - Efficient Management of Smart Street Lights 
The city uses thousands of smart streetlights with identical behavior and configuration but different 
locations. The system must minimize memory usage. 
1. Identify and justify the pattern that best optimizes resource use in this scenario. (3 marks) 
2. Explain how this pattern handles shared (intrinsic) and unique (extrinsic) properties. (4 marks) 
3. Analyze the performance impact when scaling to tens of thousands of similar objects. (8 marks) 
(Total = 15 marks) 
Part E - Interpreting City Policies Defined in DSL 
The city’s operational rules (e.g., noise limits, parking hours) are defined in a mini-language that must be 
parsed and evaluated dynamically by the system. 
1. Identify the most appropriate design pattern for interpreting this rule language. (3 marks) 
2. Provide a UML class diagram to demonstrate the selected design pattern’s mechanism. (4 marks) 
3. Critically assess the pattern’s suitability for rule updates and extensibility. (8 marks) 
(Total = 15 marks) 
Part F – Final Project Demonstration, VIVA, and Documentation 
1. You are expected to develop the system based on the given scenario, applying the appropriate 
object-oriented design patterns. 
2. You will demonstrate your application during a VIVA session, where you will explain your use 
of design patterns, discuss architectural choices, and answer questions related to the design, 
functionality, and overall system structure. (15 marks) 
3. You are expected to prepare comprehensive project documentation, including a UML class 
diagram and working code, to illustrate the design and functionality of your application. (10 
marks) 
4. You must submit a 10–15-minute recorded demonstration video showcasing your implemented 
system, its key functionalities, and the application of the selected design patterns. The video 
should clearly demonstrate how your solution addresses the given scenario.

a. Project Documentation (Project Document): Upload the final project report in PDF

b. Project Source Code (Project File): Upload the complete project source code, including 
all required files and resources, as a ZIP file to the Project File section of the Student

c. Demonstration Video: Upload the 10–15-minute recorded demonstration video in MP4
